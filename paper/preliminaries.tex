\subsection{Multi-Agent Coordination as Belief-Conditioned Sequential Decision Making}

In sequential multi-agent decision making, the consequence of an ego action
depends not only on the physical state but also on how other agents will respond
to that action. This dependence is especially explicit when their goals,
incentives, or other behaviorally relevant information are not directly
observable. We therefore adopt the interactive POMDP (I-POMDP) perspective,
which extends sequential decision making with models of other agents and maps
beliefs over interactive states to actions~\citep{gmytrasiewicz2005ipomdp}.
This is a well-established modeling structure: model the partner, predict its behavior, choose
an action under that prediction, and update the model from subsequent
interaction~\citep{carmel1996opponent}. Recent work also provides an instance of the
same overall coupling by combining inferred partner goals with
goal-conditioned policies and planning~\citep{huang2024hop}.

Let $s_t$ denote the public interaction state at decision time $t$, including the
physical state, public history, turn phase, and any pending interaction. Agent
$i$ receives observation $o_t^i$ and has a private latent state $\theta_i$;
for a partner $j$, $\theta_j$ denotes the task-relevant component of its
latent model, such as a hidden goal, incentive, or other information affecting
future behavior. We then use this perspective to separate two research objects. 
First, a \emph{belief} is the player's information
state about the feasible or plausible latent states of other players. 
Given public evidence $e_t^i$, the belief evolves through the standard model-update interface

\begin{equation}
 b_{t+1}^i=\mathsf{Update}\!\left(b_t^i,e_t^i;s_t,a_t^i\right),
 \label{eq:generic-belief-update}
\end{equation}

where $e_t^i$ may include an observed partner action and its public state
consequence.

Second, \emph{conditioned planning} evaluates candidate ego actions
using the partner model induced by the current belief. For a legal action
$a\in\mathcal A_i(s_t)$, let
$\widehat\pi_{j}^{\,i}(y\mid s_t,a,b_t^i)$ denote the resulting predictive
model for partner action or response $y$. The player then selects an action by
optimizing its expected continuation utility:

\begin{equation}
 a_t^i\in\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal A_i(s_t)}
 Q_i(s_t,b_t^i,a),\qquad
 Q_i(s_t,b_t^i,a)
 =\mathbb E\!\left[U_i(s_T;\theta_i)\mid s_t,o_t^i,b_t^i,a\right].
 \label{eq:belief-conditioned-planning}
\end{equation}

The expectation includes the predicted partner responses and subsequent
interaction dynamics. The two objects are therefore distinct but coupled:
$b_t^i$ determines the partner model used by planning, while the realized
interaction outcome supplies evidence for the next belief.

\subsection{BENAC-Based Environment and Oracle Partners}

\paragraph{Base BENAC.}
We build on BENAC, a negotiation setting in which players make sequential,
action-level commitments toward multiple goals~\citep{benac2026benchmark}.
The original implementation stores binding binary commitments in a policy
matrix, represents goals by satisfaction masks, applies an accepted bilateral
deal by a bitwise OR, and advances the schedule without changing commitments
when a deal is rejected. The extension used here preserves this commitment
semantics while making private incentives, explicit responses, menu offers, and
multi-player scheduling part of the game specification.

\paragraph{Hidden incentives.}
An instance is
$\mathcal M=(N,\mathbf K,\mathcal G,V,\rho,M,F)$, where $N\ge 2$ is the number
of players, $\mathbf K=(K_1,\ldots,K_N)$ gives the number of binary actions per
player, $\mathcal G=\{0,\ldots,G-1\}$ is the set of public goals, $\rho$ is
the public proposer schedule, $M$ is the per-side commitment-change budget,
and $F$ is an optional set of forbidden actions. The environment stores the
complete preference matrix

\begin{equation}
 V=(v_{i,g})\in\{-1,0,+1\}^{N\times G},
 \qquad
 +1=\mathrm{WANT},\quad 0=\mathrm{NEUTRAL},\quad -1=\mathrm{AVOID}.
 \label{eq:preference-matrix}
\end{equation}

Player $i$ privately observes only its own row $v_{i,:}$ in the normal private
view. For each player $i$, let
$c_{t,i}\in\{0,1\}^{K_i}$ be its binding commitments and let
$C_t=(c_{t,1},\ldots,c_{t,N})$. A public state is
$s_t=(C_t,t,\tau_t)$ together with the public game specification, where
$\tau_t$ is the transcript. Commitments are monotone: every transition
preserves existing $1$ entries and can only add $0\mathord\to1$ entries. Each
goal $g$ has a nonempty public requirement set
$\Gamma_g\subseteq\{(i,k):1\le i\le N,1\le k\le K_i\}$ and is satisfied iff

\begin{equation}
 z_g(C_t)=\mathbf 1\!\left[\,c_{t,i,k}=1\text{ for all }(i,k)\in\Gamma_g\,\right].
 \label{eq:goal-satisfaction}
\end{equation}

The implementation restricts every goal to involve at least two distinct
players. Hence, in the current partial-information setting, the hidden partner
state is the preference/incentive row rather than the goal graph: goal
requirements remain public. Player $i$'s terminal utility is the additive preference-weighted
utility

\begin{equation}
 U_i(C_T)=\sum_{g=0}^{G-1}v_{i,g}z_g(C_T).
 \label{eq:terminal-utility}
\end{equation}

The episode terminates exactly when the schedule is exhausted, $t=T=|\rho|$;
goal completion does not terminate the episode early. The runner reports the
utility at this terminal state and does not emit a separate shaped transition
reward.

\paragraph{Menu offers.}
At turn $t$, the proposer is $p=\rho_t$. An ordinary offer addressed to
$q\ne p$ is

\begin{equation}
 o=(q,\bar c_p,\bar c_q),
 \qquad \bar c_p\in\{0,1\}^{K_p},\quad
 \bar c_q\in\{0,1\}^{K_q},
 \end{equation}

where the vectors are final target vectors rather than deltas. If
$D_k=\{a:c_{t,k,a}=0,\ \bar c_{k,a}=1\}$, legality requires preservation of
existing commitments, $|D_p|\le M$, $|D_q|\le M$, no newly added forbidden
action, and $|D_p|+|D_q|>0$. The ordinary action space contains PASS and all
legal offers to every $q\ne p$. PASS leaves $C_t$ unchanged and consumes the
turn. For an ordinary offer, the partner response is
$r\in\{\textsc{ACCEPT},\textsc{REJECT}\}$. ACCEPT updates

\begin{equation}
 c_{t+1,k}=c_{t,k}\lor\bar c_k\quad(k\in\{p,q\}),
 \end{equation}

leaving all other rows unchanged; REJECT leaves $C_t$ unchanged. Both outcomes
advance the schedule cursor by exactly one and are recorded in the public
transcript.

When menus are enabled, a menu is a pair of distinct legal ordinary offers to
the same partner, $m=(o^{(1)},o^{(2)})$. Its response set is
$\{\textsc{REJECT},\textsc{CHOOSE\_1},\textsc{CHOOSE\_2}\}$; only the chosen
option binds, rejection binds neither, and the menu also consumes one
proposer turn.

\paragraph{Multi-party interaction and observation.}
The episode is multi-party even though each individual offer is bilateral:
$N$ may exceed two, a goal may require commitments from multiple players, and
commitments accumulated in different bilateral interactions jointly determine
the terminal utilities. The generator defaults to
$(N,K_i,G,R,M)=(3,3,8,4,1)$, samples a player permutation for each of $R$
rounds, and uses the concatenated schedule $\rho$; all of these dimensions and
the schedule are configurable. The schema permits $N=2$ for small games, while
the generator directly supports $N\ge3$. Each resolved proposal consumes one
scheduled turn, with a response only when the proposal is an offer.

The normal private observation of player $i$ contains the public goals and
requirements, binding commitments, schedule and cursor, public transcript,
and, at the relevant phase, legal partners/offers or a pending offer, together
with $v_{i,:}$. A pending offer is hypothetical until its response is chosen.
The public observation omits private preferences; an explicit full view exposes
all rows only for debugging. The current implementation has no free-form
message channel or explicit reveal action, so its public evidence consists of
structured proposals, responses, and the resulting commitment snapshot.

\paragraph{Belief solver.}
For ego $i$, the finite latent type space for partner $j$ is the preference-row
space $\Theta_j=\{\mathrm{WANT},\mathrm{NEUTRAL},\mathrm{AVOID}\}^{G}$. The
solver maintains joint hypotheses

\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_t^i\subseteq\prod_{j\ne i}\Theta_j,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_t^i=\left(\mathcal H_t^i,w_t^i\right),
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_{j,t}^i=\{h_j:\exists h\in\mathcal H_t^i\},
 \label{eq:joint-belief-support}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal H_{j,t}^i$ is the currently feasible set for partner $j$ and
the joint representation preserves cross-partner correlations. The concrete
\texttt{BeliefState} stores normalized log weights. The default
\texttt{ConditionalPreferencePrior} starts from label probabilities
$(0.4,0.2,0.4)$ in WANT/NEUTRAL/AVOID order and conditions on the ego's row
and the generator's validity constraints; controlled fixtures may instead
provide a finite support explicitly.

Upon a valid partner-generated proposal or response, the exact filter scores
each hypothesis with the known partner kernel and applies

\begin{equation}
 w_{t+1}^i(h)=
 \frac{w_t^i(h)\,\ell_j(e_t\mid s_t,\theta_j(h))}
 {\sum_{h'\in\mathcal H_t^i}w_t^i(h')\,\ell_j(e_t\mid s_t,\theta_j(h'))},
 \qquad h\in\mathcal H_t^i,
 \label{eq:bayes-filter}
\end{equation}

with zero-likelihood hypotheses assigned zero posterior mass. The concrete
implementation retains such hypotheses in the enumerated support with
$-\infty$ log weight. Ego
actions are interventions and do not update the likelihood. The filter replays
only unseen, valid, in-order public events and pending offers; invalid or
fallback events are marked as such and are not evidence. In the repository,
the kernel is the known stochastic \texttt{SoftProgressPolicy}, which uses the
acting player's own preference row and public state, scores actions by accepted
state potential gain (with partial-progress weight $0.5$, zero on the last
turn), and applies a temperature-$0.5$ softmax with a $0.02$ uniform mixture.
It is a predictive partner kernel, not a rational oracle and not a model of a
partner's belief about other players. The deterministic commitment snapshot is
used to validate the public transition; the likelihood is attached to the
observed proposal or response. The associated \texttt{BayesPlanner}
consumes $\mathcal B_t^i$, branches on the same partner kernel, updates the
belief at non-ego nodes, and maximizes ego terminal utility over the remaining
schedule without discount; it is therefore a belief-conditioned reference
planner rather than an equilibrium solver.

\paragraph{Oracle partners.}
An observation-limited rational partner would have the form

\begin{equation}
 a_j^t=\pi_{\mathrm{oracle}}\!\left(o_j^t,\mathcal B_{-j}^t\right),
 \label{eq:limited-oracle}
\end{equation}

where $o_j^t$ contains the public state and $j$'s own private incentive, and
$\mathcal B_{-j}^t$ is maintained from information available to $j$ when
inferring other players. Under this semantics, ground-truth access is limited
to $j$'s own private state; all inference about other agents must use the same
observation and belief-update interface as an ordinary player.

The current reference implementation should be distinguished from this
observation-limited definition. \texttt{RationalOraclePolicy} is bound by
\texttt{GameRunner} to the live state and player identity, then delegates to
\texttt{PerfectInfoSolver}. The solver reads the complete \texttt{GameSpec},
including every row of the ground-truth $V$, and performs finite-horizon
backward induction. For a pending ordinary offer $o$ to responder $q$, let
$W_q^{\mathrm{acc}}(s,o)$ and $W_q^{\mathrm{rej}}(s,o)$ denote the continuation
utilities obtained by accepting and rejecting. Its response is

\begin{equation}
 r_q^{\mathrm{PI}}(s,o)=
 \begin{cases}
 \textsc{ACCEPT}, & W_q^{\mathrm{acc}}(s,o)\ge W_q^{\mathrm{rej}}(s,o),\\
 \textsc{REJECT}, & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:perfect-info-response}
\end{equation}

At proposer nodes it chooses an accepted offer maximizing the proposer's
continuation utility; PASS is the baseline and wins ties, with remaining ties
resolved by legal enumeration order. This solver is menu-disabled. It does not
maintain a belief about the ego, and the observation passed to the policy is
used only for identity and turn-consistency checks; legality is checked against
the bound live state rather than used to restrict the solver's access to $V$.

For clarity, the implementation exposes four distinct objects: (i) the
\emph{environment ground truth}, comprising the full $V$, public specification,
and current physical state; (ii) the \emph{agent-observable information},
comprising the public state plus the agent's own private row in private mode;
(iii) the \emph{solver-maintained belief}, comprising the current feasible
partner hypotheses and weights; and (iv) the \emph{policy}, which maps the
relevant information to an action. The exact filter and \texttt{BayesPlanner}
use the belief object, whereas the current \texttt{RationalOraclePolicy} is a
full-information reference and must not be interpreted as an
observation-limited oracle.
