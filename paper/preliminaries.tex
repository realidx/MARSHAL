\subsection{Multi-Agent Coordination as Belief-Conditioned Decision Making}
\label{sec:coordination-formulation}

Multi-agent coordination requires an agent to anticipate how other agents will
act when selecting its own action, especially when their objectives,
incentives, or relevant information are not directly observable.  Prior work
on opponent modeling addresses this challenge by representing other agents
with explicit behavioral models and inferring their actions from interaction
histories~\citep{carmel1996opponent}. Existing work formalize this setting as an
interactive partially observable Markov decision process (I-POMDP), in which
the interactive state includes models of other agents and observations update
beliefs over those models~\citep{gmytrasiewicz2005ipomdp}.

\paragraph{Interactive model.}
Following the I-POMDP definition, the direct $N$-agent form for
agent $i$ is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{I\mbox{-}POMDP}_i
 =\left\langle IS_i,A,T_i,\Omega_i,O_i,R_i\right\rangle,
 \qquad
 IS_i=S\times\prod_{j\ne i}M_j,
 \qquad
 A=\prod_{j\in\mathcal N}A_j,
 \label{eq:ipomdp-definition}
\end{equation}
[TODO: put the formal definition in the Appendix]
where $S$ is the physical state space, $M_j$ is the model space for agent
$j$, $A$ is the joint action space, and $T_i$, $O_i$, and $R_i$ are the
transition, observation, and reward specifications.  In the intentional model
case, a model of agent $j$ is a type
$\theta_j=\langle \widetilde b^{\,j},\widehat\theta_j\rangle$, where
$\widetilde b^{\,j}$ is the belief state attributed to $j$ and
$\widehat\theta_j$ is its frame, including its observation, action,
transition, reward, and optimality specifications.  Throughout the remainder
of this section, $b_t^i\in\Delta(IS_i)$ denotes only the time-indexed belief
maintained by agent $i$ over its interactive state space; the tilde-marked
$\widetilde b^{\,j}$ is used only for a belief nested inside a hypothesized
model of another agent.  Under model non-manipulability (MNM), agent $i$'s
actions do not directly modify agent $j$'s model.  Under model
non-observability (MNO), agent $i$ cannot directly inspect that model.
Accordingly, beliefs about other-agent models can change only through
observations generated by the environment and by other agents' behavior,
although an agent may still influence those observations indirectly through
the public state.

The belief-state estimator is the Bayesian update operator $\tau_i$.  Given
agent $i$'s current belief, action, and subsequent observation, it propagates
the belief through the physical transition and the action models of the other
agents, conditions on the observation likelihood, and normalizes the result:
\begin{equation}
 b_{t+1}^i
 =\tau_i\!\left(b_t^i,a_t^i,o_{t+1}^i\right),
 \label{eq:ipomdp-belief-update}
\end{equation}
For intentional models, the action prediction may itself invoke the belief
update specified by the modeled agent.  For a candidate action $a_t^i$, let
$\pi_{-i}(\cdot\mid x,a_t^i)$ denote the joint response distribution induced
by the other-agent models at interactive state $x$.  The belief-averaged
immediate reward is the expectation
\begin{equation}
 \bar R_i(b_t^i,a_t^i)
 =\mathbb E_{\substack{x\sim b_t^i\\
                 a_{-i}\sim\pi_{-i}(\cdot\mid x,a_t^i)}}
    \left[R_i\!\left(x,(a_t^i,a_{-i})\right)\right].
 \label{eq:belief-averaged-reward}
\end{equation}
Here, ``averaged'' means expectation under the current belief and the
response distribution predicted by the supported models, rather than an
arithmetic average.  This is the standard belief-state notation for the
immediate reward; the same predictive distribution induces the observation
probabilities in the standard belief-state Bellman recursion below:
\begin{equation}
 V_i(b_t^i)=\max_{a_t^i\in A_i}\left[
 \bar R_i(b_t^i,a_t^i)
 +\gamma\sum_{o_{t+1}^i}
 \Pr(o_{t+1}^i\mid b_t^i,a_t^i)
 V_i\!\left(\tau_i(b_t^i,a_t^i,o_{t+1}^i)\right)
 \right].
 \label{eq:ipomdp-value-recursion}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Partner belief and conditioned planning.}

We call the portion of $b_t^i$ that concerns the behaviorally relevant
components of $M_j$ a \emph{partner belief}.  Such a component may encode a
hidden goal, incentive, behavior, or relevant knowledge of
agent $j$. \emph{Partner-conditioned planning} is the decision making operation that
uses this belief.  For a candidate action $a_t^i$, agent $i$ evaluates the
responses of the other agents under the models supported by $b_t^i$, applies
the resulting state transition, and accounts for downstream outcome before
selecting an action.  The action may be a task action, a proposal addressed
to a particular agent, or a response to an offer.  In this sense, the belief
determines the partner model used in planning, while planning determines
which action is taken and hence which responses and observations can occur.

\paragraph{Commitments, goals, and utility.}
At turn $t$, the commitment state is
$C_t=(c_{t,i})_{i\in\mathcal N}$ with
$c_{t,i}\in\{0,1\}^{K_i}$.  Commitments are irreversible, which means one entry cannot
be unset by a later action. With $\delta_g\in\{\mathrm{bin},\mathrm{lin}\}$
denoting the goal mode, its satisfaction is
\begin{equation}
 S_g(C_t)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathbf 1\!\left[c_{t,i,k}=1\text{ for every }(i,k)\in\Gamma_g\right],
     &\delta_g=\mathrm{bin},\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma_g|}
 \sum_{(i,k)\in\Gamma_g}c_{t,i,k},
     &\delta_g=\mathrm{lin}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:goal-satisfaction}
\end{equation}
Each goal involves at least two players.  The environment samples a private
preference matrix $V=(v_{i,g})$ with
$v_{i,g}\in\{-1,0,+1\}$, corresponding to \textsc{Avoid},
\textsc{Neutral}, and \textsc{Want}.  Player $i$ observes only its own row
$v_i$.  At termination, after all turns in $\rho$ have been consumed, its
utility is $U_i(C_T;v_i)=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}v_{i,g}S_g(C_T)$ [TODO: explan use one sentence how the utility is given]

\paragraph{Native actions and transitions.}
Let $p=\rho_t$ be the current proposer and let
$s_t=(C_t,t,H_t)$ denote the public physical state, turn index, and public
transcript.  A proposer may choose \textsc{Pass} or an ordinary offer to any
$q\ne p$.  An offer is $o=(q,\bar c_p,\bar c_q)$.
Existing commitments must remain set; each side may add
at most $M$ new commitments; forbidden commitments may
not be added; and an offer must add at least one commitment in total.

For an ordinary offer, the addressed player chooses \textsc{Accept} or
\textsc{Reject}.  Acceptance binds the proposed commitments immediately:
\begin{equation}
 c_{t+1,i}=
 \begin{cases}
 c_{t,i}\lor\bar c_i, & i\in\{p,q\},\\
 c_{t,i}, & \text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:offer-transition}
\end{equation}
whereas rejection leaves $C_t$ unchanged.  A completed proposal--response
interaction is recorded in the public transcript and advances the schedule by
one proposer turn.  \textsc{Pass} advances the schedule immediately without
changing commitments.

\paragraph{Belief filtering.}
For agent $i$, let
$h=(v_j)_{j\ne i}$ be a joint hypothesis about the other players' private
rows.  Let $\mathcal V_j$ denote the public finite catalogue of candidate rows
for player $j$; in the native diagnostic,
$\mathcal H_t^i\subseteq\prod_{j\ne i}\mathcal V_j$ is the currently retained
support.  A declared prior is conditioned on $v_i$ and any supplied initial
support.  If
agent $i$ observes a public action $u_t^j$ by player $j$ (a proposal or a
response), the general finite-support update is
\begin{equation}
 b_{t+1}^i(h)=
 \frac{b_t^i(h)\,\ell_j\!\left(u_t^j\mid I_t^j,h\right)}
 {\displaystyle\sum_{h'\in\mathcal H_t^i}
  b_t^i(h')\,\ell_j\!\left(u_t^j\mid I_t^j,h'\right)},
 \qquad h\in\mathcal H_t^i,
 \label{eq:bayes-filter}
\end{equation}
where $I_t^j$ contains the information available to the acting player and
$\ell_j$ is the likelihood induced by its declared policy.  A deterministic
policy gives
$\ell_j(u\mid I_t^j,h)=\mathbf 1[f_j(I_t^j,h)=u]$, so the update reduces
to support filtering,
\[
 \mathcal H_{t+1}^i
 =\{h\in\mathcal H_t^i:f_j(I_t^j,h)=u_t^j\}.
\]
For a kernel that depends only on the acting player's row, the likelihood
reduces to a function of $h_j$.  The active oracle used in the native
diagnostic may also depend on its inferred distribution over the other rows;
in that case the dependence on the full $h$ in \eqref{eq:bayes-filter}
captures the acting player's own information state.
The solver treats agent $i$'s own actions as interventions rather than as
likelihood evidence.  A public commitment transition is used to check the
history and update the physical state; it does not by itself reveal a private
row.  The repository's general \texttt{ExactBayesFilter} implements the
weighted version for known \texttt{SoftProgressPolicy} kernels, while the
native diagnostic uses the deterministic support form over explicit,
independent per-player catalogues.  Thus, in the latter setting, any
cross-player dependence in a posterior comes from observed public behavior or
conditioning, not from a shared type label.

\paragraph{Oracle partners.}
The native diagnostic uses \texttt{RationalPartner} as an information-limited
oracle.  At a proposal or response decision, player $j$ is supplied with its
public information
$I_t^j=(s_t,H_t,\text{pending offer},v_j,\mathcal A_t^j)$ and chooses from
the complete legal native action set according to
\begin{equation}
 \pi_j^{\mathrm{orc}}(u\mid I_t^j)
 \in \argmax_{u\in\mathcal A_t^j}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 U_j(C_T;v_j)\mid I_t^j,u,\pi_{-j}^{\mathrm{ref}}
 \right].
 \label{eq:oracle-partner}
\end{equation}
The expectation is over the other players' independent public type
catalogues, conditioned on $j$'s own row and the public history.  The oracle
enumerates the remaining original schedule, propagates the exact binding
commitment transitions, and evaluates terminal utility.  Its fixed reference
continuation uses the code's immediate rule: a responder chooses the action
with the greatest currently satisfied own-goal utility, with rejection winning
ties; a proposer chooses the ordinary offer with greatest immediate own
utility under acceptance, with \textsc{Pass} winning ties.  The reference
rule does not propose menus, although the terminal best-response search
enumerates all legal offers, menus, and responses.

Past actions by oracle players are replayed under their actual oracle policy
when a counterfactual information state is constructed; actions taken by the
evaluated player are interventions and are not treated as evidence about its
own hidden row.  If a policy requires an inference about another player, that
inference is recomputed from the acting player's public history and catalogue,
not replaced by the environment's realized private information.  The
implementation has no stochastic action error.  The resulting policy is an
exact best response to the declared reference continuation, not an equilibrium
or a full-information solution.  The legacy \texttt{PerfectInfoSolver} is a
separate full-information mechanism-checking tool and is not the oracle used
for menu-enabled native endgames.

\paragraph{Reference planning.}
At a decision of player $i$, the exact search enumerates the legal action set
for the current role, simulates the intervening oracle actions, updates the
compatible support, and replans at the next decision of player $i$.  Let $e$
denote the intervening public evidence, $s_e$ the resulting physical state,
and $\tau_i$ the update in \eqref{eq:bayes-filter}.  The finite-horizon
reference recursion is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 W_i(s,H,b)&=\max_{a\in\mathcal A_i(s,H)}
 \sum_e p_b(e\mid s,H,a)\,
 W_i\!\left(s_e,H\mathbin{\oplus}(a,e),
            \tau_i(b,H,a,e)\right),\\
 W_i(s_T,H,b)&=U_i(C_T;v_i).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:benac-reference-planner}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal A_i(s,H)$ is a proposal set when $i$ proposes and a response
set when $i$ is responding.  Response decisions are therefore not folded
into an automatic approval step.  The native endgame search stops a branch at
the next such decision or at termination, while all intervening partner
actions retain their probabilities or deterministic support weights.  The
planner optimizes terminal utility under the specified partner policy; it
does not optimize information gain as a separate objective and does not
access the realized private preference rows.
