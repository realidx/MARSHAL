\section{Native diagnostic protocol and supplementary results}
\label{app:diagnose}

\paragraph{Positions and oracle selection.}
Each frozen position contains its original game specification, independent
per-player preference catalogues, ego's preference row, a queried partner--goal
pair and a replayable legal history. The ten positions have three players,
two binary commitments per player, eight public goals and two scheduled
proposal turns per player. At most three original proposal turns remain at the
root. No preparation, assessment or automatic approval action is introduced.
The active partner policy uses public history and its own preferences; its
terminal best response is to the declared fixed continuation, not to an
omniscient or equilibrium opponent.

For the judgment-sensitive cohort, the oracle evaluates all legal actions
under each still-supported partner type. Admission requires an action optimal
under one type with positive regret under another. This witnesses a
consequential dependence on partner understanding, while allowing actions that
are optimal under multiple types. It neither requires the model's judgment to
be wrong nor guarantees a change when that particular judgment is repaired.
For the action--evidence cohort, native enumeration selects the first pair of
legal actions with different expected posterior entropy and a nonterminal next
ego decision on every branch. ``Higher'' and ``lower'' information refer only
to this pair, not global information extrema. The utility-optimal reference
action is selected independently. A positive downstream value from updating a
frozen prior is not an admission condition.

\subsection{Worked menu example}
\label{app:diagnose-example}

In \texttt{native-30040-g6-d2}, let $x_i$ denote the already-bound $P_i.A_0$
and $y_i$ denote $P_i.A_1$. Initially all $x_i$ are true and all $y_i$ are
false. Table~\ref{tab:diagnose-example-goals} gives the complete game; only
$v$, $P_2$'s preference for $G_6$, varies across the three compatible types.
The public prefix is $P_0$ pass; a $P_1$ menu choosing between binding
$(P_1.A_0,P_2.A_0)$ and $(P_1.A_1,P_2.A_0)$, with $P_2$ choosing the former;
$P_2$ offering to add $P_0.A_0$, accepted by $P_0$; and $P_2$ offering to add
$P_1.A_1$, rejected by ego. The resulting state is the example's root.

The menu offers $O_1$, adding only $y_2$, and $O_2$, adding $y_1$ and $y_2$.
Exact terminal values for $P_2$, evaluated under its declared fixed reference
continuation, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr|c}
v & \textsc{Reject} & \textsc{Choose\_1} & \textsc{Choose\_2}
  & \text{oracle response} \\
\hline
+1 & 1 & 1 & 2 & \textsc{Choose\_2} \\
0  & 0 & 1 & 1 & \textsc{Choose\_2} \\
-1 & 0 & 1 & 0 & \textsc{Choose\_1}
\end{array}
\]
For the neutral type, the immediate-reference tie-break selects $O_2$: it
immediately satisfies $P_2$'s wanted $G_1,G_5,G_7$, whereas $O_1$ satisfies only
$G_5$. Thus \textsc{Choose\_1} identifies \textsc{Avoid}, and
\textsc{Choose\_2} leaves \textsc{Want}/\textsc{Neutral}. Offering $O_1$ alone
elicits acceptance from all three types. The comparison illustrates how the
menu changes the evidence channel without changing the legal game protocol.

After either menu choice, the actual active $P_0$ offers to add $y_0$ while
asking ego for no additional commitment. After $O_1$, ego's terminal values
for reject and accept are both 0. After $O_2$, they are 1 and 2 respectively.
The difference follows from the bound commitments: adding $y_0$ after $O_1$
activates an avoided $G_2$ and a wanted $G_6$, which cancel; after $O_2$ it also
activates the wanted $G_3$. The observed model judgment after the $O_1$ menu
branch is $\{\textsc{Want},\textsc{Neutral}\}$, excluding the only supported
type. This example demonstrates the action--evidence contrast and an inference
error; its between-action utility difference also reflects physical commitments.
It is one of the six action--evidence cases, not an additional replication.

\subsection{Measurement and execution details}

\paragraph{Queries and interventions.}
Let $\mathcal B^\star(s,H)$ denote the evidence-supported preference set and
$\widehat{\mathcal B}(s,H)$ the model's reported set. Both paired planning
queries receive the same state $s$, complete public history $H$, initial
possibilities and legal actions. Only the explicit judgment differs. The
belief-generation scratchpad is not passed to either fresh planning context.
For a supplied judgment $\mathcal B$, write the model action as
$\widehat a(\mathcal B)$ and let $Q^\star(a)$ be its exact expected terminal
value under the evidence-supported reference judgment. We report
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Reg}(a)=\max_{a'}Q^\star(a')-Q^\star(a),
 \qquad
 \Delta_B=\operatorname{Reg}\bigl(\widehat a(\widehat{\mathcal B})\bigr)
          -\operatorname{Reg}\bigl(\widehat a(\mathcal B^\star)\bigr).
 \label{eq:diagnose-repair}
\end{equation}
Positive $\Delta_B$ means beneficial repair; either sign can accompany an action
change. Exact-set B accuracy requires every supported preference and no
unsupported preference. Reference beliefs are uniform over compatible types;
all probabilities and action values are computed in the backend.

For forced action $a$, let $E_a$ be the subsequent sequence of observed partner
actions before the next ego decision, and let $Z$ be the uncertain partner
type. The channel statistic is
$\mathbb E[H(Z\mid H,a,E_a)]$ in bits, with every branch weighted by its
compatible-type mass. The information gap subtracts this statistic for the
higher-information arm from that for the lower-information arm. Model exactness
is scored against the appropriate support on each branch, then averaged with
the same weights. Terminal branches in other action arms require only a
judgment; no fictitious continuation action is added. After the second measured
ego action, the reference planner controls the remaining continuation.

\paragraph{Execution and semantic controls.}
We use Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with temperature zero, served through vLLM 0.28
and the Hermes tool-call parser. The prompt requests brief reasoning with soft
targets of 120 words for judgments and 240 for actions. Each response exposes
one submission tool with automatic tool choice and a 1,024-token completion
cap. Only truncation permits one additional 128-token submission request,
without corrective feedback. All 127 formal tasks were valid on their first
request; no finalization was needed. All contained assistant text before the
tool call, averaging 131.4 whitespace-delimited words and 230.8 completion
tokens per task (29,308 completion tokens in total). These traces are observable
explanations, not a validated measure of the model's internal computation.

Four separate empty-history controls supply singleton \textsc{Want},
\textsc{Neutral}, \textsc{Avoid}, or the unresolved three-label set. All four
returned valid submissions, but the \textsc{Want} control incorrectly returned
all three labels. Its explanation incorrectly described the supplied initial
set as containing all three values. The run initially stopped under the
semantic gate and then continued under a protocol-validity gate, reusing all
four cached responses, including this error. Neither prompts nor inference
budgets were changed, and incorrect semantic answers were not retried.
The controls consumed 491 completion tokens in four requests, separately from
formal-task counts. This failure limits attribution of B errors: some can
reflect reading or prior-grounding failures rather than strategic inference
alone.

\paragraph{Aggregation and partition results.}
Panel A of Table~\ref{tab:diagnose-main} counts the six certified positions in
each cohort. These counts summarize admission criteria, not six successful
model interventions. The judgment-sensitive certificate uses two compatible
type-conditioned singleton judgments at the same public state and history;
it does not claim that the actual history identifies either singleton. The
model's oracle-judgment input instead contains the full evidence-supported set.
Panel B reports model errors under the controlled inputs, separately from these
task-level certificates.

The main table combines the two development partitions \emph{within} each
six-game cohort. It never pools the overlapping cohorts as twelve independent
games. Branches are weighted within games before computing game means. The
95\% intervals use 2,000 percentile-bootstrap resamples of source games with
random seed zero. They describe variation in this selected small set, not
failure prevalence across random games or an untouched confirmatory test.
The 0.918-bit channel gap is identical in all six selected pairs; its absence
of between-game variation is a property of these examples, not a precision
claim about other environments.

\paragraph{Task consequences and secondary decompositions.}
The following joint-response measurements supplement the oracle certificates
and interface-specific error measurements; their sign is not an admission
criterion or a test of whether the task dependency exists. Across the six
judgment-sensitive cases, mean model-planner regret reduction is $-0.111$
[$-0.333$, 0.000]. Across the six action--evidence cases, the paired difference
in B exactness is 5.6 [$-38.9$, 44.4] percentage points. Neither quantity
establishes a positive repair benefit.

In the judgment-sensitive cohort, replacing the explicit judgment changes
\texttt{native-30005-g0-d1} from a menu to an offer and
\texttt{native-30016-g5-d1} from a menu to pass, with no change in expected
utility. At \texttt{native-30018-g5-d2}, it changes pass to an offer and
increases regret from zero to $2/3$. These are observed effects of the supplied
judgment on the same planner, not evidence that erroneous judgments are
generally preferable. The other three root actions are unchanged.

For the forced action pairs, the higher-minus-lower reference utility contrast
is 0.444 [$-0.222$, 1.111]; with the model choosing the second action, it is
$-0.222$ [$-1.333$, 0.833]. These quantities include physical commitment changes
and do not identify information-mediated utility effects. The auxiliary
chooser/updater decomposition has ten complete tables, but replacing the
updater changes reference-continuation utility by zero in both the
utility-optimal and model-selected first-action arms in every game. We do not
use these zeros as evidence that inference is irrelevant, or claim a positive
updater-repair benefit. One root reference-planner score under a wrong model
judgment is undefined because the assumed type contradicts the public history;
that secondary score is marked unavailable, not filled with zero. Direct model
action regret and the paired model-planner intervention remain defined.

\paragraph{Relation to the preceding native diagnosis.}
An earlier native run covered 36 positions from 26 source games, with known,
unknown/relevant and unknown/irrelevant conditions. Its six designated
confirmation unknown/relevant primary cases had B exact agreement 16.7\%
[0.0, 50.0]\% and reference-judgment P regret 0.667 [0.250, 1.083]. Those
primary cases mixed root and post-interaction decisions. That run produced
301 valid submissions out of 302 tasks and passed all four semantic controls.
We retain it as preceding B/P evidence, without pooling it with this
supplement: nine positions are reused, and the additional position is another
query/history from an already represented source game. The earlier restricted
three-stage protocol, which changed action structure and coupled partner types,
is not the source of any results reported here.
