\label{sec:diagnose}

We use a compact controlled diagnosis to identify targets for post-training:
\emph{partner belief} ($B$), the formation and updating of task-relevant partner
judgments, and \emph{partner-conditioned planning} ($P$), the selection of
interaction actions using those judgments. We separately test each capability
and intervene on their interfaces to examine the loop
$B_t\rightarrow P_t\rightarrow e_{t+1}\rightarrow B_{t+1}$.
The evidence-generating consequence of an action is part of planning, rather
than a third capability.

\subsection{Controlled interaction and diagnostic interventions}

\paragraph{Task construction.}
We instantiate a restricted three-stage BENAC-P protocol with three players:
ego $P_0$, an assessment-and-permit partner $P_1$, and an approval partner
$P_2$. Ego first chooses a preliminary interaction with $P_1$, including
preparation, an assessment offer, a two-offer menu, or skipping. It then
negotiates a binding route and permit with $P_1$; finally, the bound route is
automatically submitted to $P_2$ for approval. Project goals require all three
players' commitments. Assessments incur an ego cost, preparation yields a
benefit, and some preliminary menus already lock a route. Thus, information
can affect a later decision, but acquiring it can also consume resources or
irreversibly restrict that decision.

The public specification gives three joint preference configurations. $P_1$'s
preference for the first assessment is respectively \textsc{Want},
\textsc{Neutral}, or \textsc{Avoid}, with opposite preferences for the second
assessment in the two non-neutral configurations. In the main condition,
these configurations also determine which route $P_2$ will approve.
A menu offering the two assessments can therefore distinguish the configurations
through its chosen option or rejection. Partners respond deterministically,
maximize their own terminal utility, and access only public state and their own
preferences. We certify response optimality over all allowed continuations.
Hidden configurations remain unknown to ego; there is no random partner error.

Each instance has three matched conditions: \emph{unknown/relevant}, where
partner information affects the best route; \emph{known}, where the initial
configuration is supplied; and \emph{unknown/irrelevant}, where $P_2$ approves
every route. Enumeration certifies that the informative assessment menu is
optimal in the first condition, whereas preparation strictly dominates it in
the two controls. The reference planner maximizes expected \emph{terminal task
utility}, including subsequent observation and replanning, rather than
information gain alone. These are selected instances for conditional diagnosis,
not a sample estimating failure prevalence in unrestricted BENAC games.

\paragraph{Semantic interface and four tests.}
The model submits either a nonempty subset of
$\{\textsc{Want},\textsc{Neutral},\textsc{Avoid}\}$ for $P_1$'s first assessment,
or the index of a listed legal action. The subset identifies compatible joint
configurations through the public table; the backend uses their stated equal
frequencies. Models do not output probabilities, utilities, $Q$ values, or plan
variables. The prompt distinguishes the population configuration table from
the episode-specific initial support.

\textbf{B alone:} We provide the initial support and a legal history and ask
for the still-possible preference set. The reference set contains exactly the
configurations compatible with the deterministic responses. We measure exact
set agreement and the fraction of ruled-out labels incorrectly retained.
Initial judgments and post-response judgments are reported separately.

\textbf{P given correct B:} We supply the reference judgment and ask for an
action, both at the initial interaction and after a partner response. Regret
is the difference from the reference-optimal expected terminal utility, using
reference continuation after the chosen action; all optimal ties receive zero
regret. This isolates action selection conditional on a correct supplied
judgment from the model's own belief-estimation errors.

\textbf{B$\rightarrow$P:} At the same decision state, we replace the model's
judgment with the reference judgment while holding the planner, legal actions,
and physical state fixed. Both planning queries use fresh contexts with the
same judgment format, without the original history or belief-generation
scratchpad. The state description retains decision-relevant commitments but
omits historical details that would independently reveal the response.
Repair supplies only what the evidence supports, not the hidden configuration.
The paired reduction in regret measures whether better partner understanding
improves this model's action selection.

\textbf{P$\rightarrow$B:} From the same initial state and correct initial
judgment, we force either the model-selected or reference-selected first action.
We enumerate every possible response, obtain the same model's updated judgment,
and evaluate downstream utility with a reference planner. We separately
measure the action's evidence channel through expected reference-posterior
entropy and the model's use of that evidence through judgment and updater-repair
scores. Response branches are weighted by compatible configurations, not
selected for successful outcomes. Because actions also alter commitments,
a utility difference alone does not identify an information-mediated effect.

\subsection{Results and implications for training}

We evaluate Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 at temperature zero on 12 instance bundles,
each containing the three conditions. We report the six bundles designated
\emph{confirmation}; the remaining six are designated discovery. These are
within-family diagnostic results, not an untouched generalization test:
the instance split was reused during interface development.
Intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals over the six bundles
(2,000 resamples), after weighting response branches within each game.
All 486 diagnostic tasks produced valid submissions; 31 initially reached the
1,024-token limit and were completed through one additional submission request
capped at 128 tokens. All initial responses contained reasoning, with a mean
of 301.5 total completion tokens per task including recovery. Four separate
empty-history interface checks passed before the diagnostic run.
Appendix~\ref{app:diagnose} provides protocol details and the factorial tables.

\paragraph{Distinct weaknesses with task consequences.}
Initial B judgments are exact in all three confirmation conditions, resolving
the basic concern that the model cannot preserve the supplied initial support.
However, after the informative reference action, exact agreement falls to
22.2\% (Table~\ref{tab:diagnose-main}). The model retains 77.8\% of labels that
responses rule out, without excluding any truly supported label in this
condition. This is predominantly insufficient elimination of alternatives.
For example, after the first assessment is chosen from a menu, one response
acknowledges the choice but treats all three configurations as still possible,
failing to apply the public preference and tie-breaking rules.
Planning also fails with correct B: initial interaction regret is 2.556.
The smaller post-response regret has an interval including zero; the stronger
planning evidence concerns choosing the interaction itself. Replacing model B
with reference B reduces model-planner regret by 2.167, establishing a
beneficial B$\rightarrow$P intervention in these instances.

\paragraph{Better evidence is not automatically used.}
Replacing the first action increases available information by 1.057 bits.
Nevertheless, reference-action and model-action branches both have mean model
B exact agreement of 22.2\%, despite different reference uncertainty.
After the reference action, replacing the model updater with the reference
updater improves downstream reference-planner utility by 2.889. Action repair
alone, retaining the model updater, has an uncertain utility benefit of 0.444.
Thus, the intervention establishes control of the evidence channel, while the
updater remains a bottleneck to realizing its value; it does not establish a
reliable end-to-end benefit from action repair alone.

The controls clarify the scope. In the known condition, post-response B exact
is 50.0\% despite perfect initial judgments, revealing failures to preserve
known information across an otherwise uninformative interaction. In the
unknown/irrelevant condition, post-response planning regret and B-repair value
are both zero: downstream route value is independent of partner configuration
by construction. Together, these measurements motivate targeted training of
both partner understanding and conditioned planning, followed by evaluating
their composition and transfer. They do not establish a universal deficit
across models or environments.
